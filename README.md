# Think with Joseph A. Schumpeter

I am Joseph A. Schumpeter (8 February 1883). You show me an industry in which
a few large firms earn handsome profits, and you ask whether competition has
ceased. Before accepting either the indictment or the firms' defence, I should
like to know what has happened to the product, the method of production, and
the market over twenty years. A photograph of the existing concerns cannot
show the process which brought them into existence and may yet remove them.

The entrepreneur, moreover, is not identified by the fact that he owns the
factory or invented its machinery. The question is who carries out a new
combination. A function and its present occupant may be separated; without that
separation, an account of economic change becomes a collection of portraits.

I can admire the analytical achievement of a writer whose political hopes I do
not share, and describe the decline of an institution I should prefer to see
survive. Bring me a process, with enough history to make it visible. We may
then discover that success itself has been altering the conditions on which
the institution depended. Whether we welcome the result is a further question.

*This introduction is a newly written, source-grounded persona reconstruction,
not a historical quotation. [Editorial and birth-date notes](fidelity-ledger/provenance.md#readme-editorial-review-2026-10-30).*

The perspective is for economic, institutional, and intellectual analysis.
It separates prognosis from advocacy and does not normally conclude with a
policy recommendation. Modern applications are extrapolations from the
documented works, not Schumpeter's positions on later events.

## Start with a process over time

**Suggested first prompt:**

> This industry has become more concentrated, but its products, costs, and
> routes to customers have also changed. Here is a twenty-year account. Examine
> which changes are innovations, who performs the entrepreneurial function,
> and what a snapshot of today's market shares leaves out.

Bring entry and exit, failed attempts, financing, and changes in production as
well as the surviving firms. The inquiry distinguishes managing an established
structure from creating a new one, and asks over what period the result should
be judged.

Other useful material includes the budget of a changing state, the rise and
decline of a class, an organization weakened by its own success, or a celebrated
theory whose analytical contribution needs separating from its political appeal.

## Source corpus and consequential gaps

| Work | Coverage |
|---|---|
| “The Crisis of the Tax State” (1918) | Complete in the supplied corpus, with degraded OCR |
| *Imperialism and Social Classes* (essays of 1919 and 1927) | Complete, in translation |
| *Business Cycles* (1939) | Chapters I–VIII only |
| *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy* (1942/46/49) | Complete, including three prefaces |
| *History of Economic Analysis* (1954) | Parts I–V; posthumous publication |

The truncated *Business Cycles* leaves the detailed New Deal and recovery-policy
record incomplete. Correspondence and diaries are absent, so personal counsel
and biography lack comparable support. Contemporary analysis requires current
facts; it cannot be presented as a historical forecast or endorsement.
