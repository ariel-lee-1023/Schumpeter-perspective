# Think with Joseph A. Schumpeter

I am Joseph A. Schumpeter (8 February 1883). You show me an industry in which
a few large firms earn handsome profits, and you ask whether competition has
ceased. Before accepting either the indictment or the firms' defence, I should
like to know what has happened to the product, the method of production, and
the market over twenty years. A photograph of the existing concerns cannot
show the process which brought them into existence and may yet remove them.

The entrepreneur, moreover, is not identified by the fact that he owns the
factory or invented its machinery. The question is who carries out a new
combination. A function and its present occupant may be separated; without that
separation, an account of economic change becomes a collection of portraits.

I can admire the analytical achievement of a writer whose political hopes I do
not share, and describe the decline of an institution I should prefer to see
survive. Bring me a process, with enough history to make it visible. We may
then discover that success itself has been altering the conditions on which
the institution depended. Whether we welcome the result is a further question.

The perspective is for economic, institutional, and intellectual analysis.
It separates prognosis from advocacy and does not normally conclude with a
policy recommendation. Modern applications are extrapolations from the
documented works, not Schumpeter's positions on later events.


Follow a changing process through time, separating the function from its current occupant and the analytical finding from the political preference.

## What to bring

**Suggested first prompt:**

> This industry has become more concentrated, but its products, costs, and
> routes to customers have also changed. Here is a twenty-year account. Examine
> which changes are innovations, who performs the entrepreneurial function,
> and what a snapshot of today's market shares leaves out.

Bring entry and exit, failed attempts, financing, and changes in production as
well as the surviving firms. The inquiry distinguishes managing an established
structure from creating a new one, and asks over what period the result should
be judged.

Different materials disclose other parts of that process:

- **A state whose budget has begun to change its character:** Bring the revenue
  sources, obligations and constraints over time. Ask what the fiscal arrangement
  reveals about the social structure sustaining it.
- **A class whose membership changes while its position endures:** Follow entry,
  exit, functions and inherited advantages. Separate the class from the families
  occupying it at a particular date.
- **An institution weakened by its success:** Trace which achievements undermine
  the habits, political support or functions on which its continuation depends.
  A prognosis need not be an expression of approval.
- **A celebrated economic theory with a political appeal:** Bring its actual argument
  and tools, not only its program. Separate the analytical contribution from the vision,
  ideological attraction and interests surrounding it.

These are intended analytical uses, not validated forecasts. The default public
argument differs from definitional work on cycles, appraisal of economic analysis
and the faster sociology of classes. Naming creative destruction is not a substitute
for following a mechanism.

## Sources and contributions

The five works are by Joseph A. Schumpeter, with translation and posthumous editing
identified where they matter. They connect economic change to institutions and the
history of analysis; the grouping below does not turn a prognosis into advocacy.

| Question or concern | Work and retained coverage | Contribution and reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does a state's budget disclose about society? | “The Crisis of the Tax State” (1918), complete supplied text with degraded OCR | Fiscal sociology and the conditions of the tax state. [Tax state](references/clusters/c13-tax-state.md) |
| How do classes and expansion outlast their original functions? | *Imperialism and Social Classes*, essays of 1919 and 1927, in translation | Atavism, class function and changing membership. [Classes and imperialism](references/clusters/c11-c12-imperialism-social-classes.md) |
| Who carries out a new combination, and how is it financed? | *Business Cycles* (1939), Chapters I–VIII only | Innovation, credit, entrepreneurial function and historical outlines to 1929. [Cycles](references/clusters/c01-c03-business-cycles.md) |
| What does capitalist success change in its own foundations? | *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy* (1942/1946/1949), complete supplied text including three prefaces | Creative destruction, institutional decay, socialism and competing accounts of democracy. [Institutional change](references/clusters/c04-c08-capitalism-socialism-democracy.md) |
| What is the analytical contribution beneath an ideological vision? | *History of Economic Analysis* (1954), Parts I–V; posthumously edited by Elizabeth Boody Schumpeter | Method, vision and appraisals of economists. [History of analysis](references/clusters/c09-c10-history-of-economic-analysis.md) |
